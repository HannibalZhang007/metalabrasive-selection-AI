Grinding steel shot: applications and specifications by industry

Grinding steel shot splits into two families that are not interchangeable: cleaning grades that remove rust and paint, and peening grades that induce compressive stress for fatigue life. This is which grade goes to which industry — and why the cheapest media is rarely the cheapest line.

By Hannibal Zhang, FENGERDA Updated 19 Oct 2026 Source: detecting...

Two families, two purposes

Grinding steel shot comes in two functional families. The cleaning grades (G30 / G45 / G60) remove rust, scale, paint and burrs. The peening grades (G70 / 82B / 9mm) work by impact to induce residual compressive stress, which improves fatigue life. Specifying the wrong family is the most common selection error, so start there before choosing a grade.

Grinding steel shot for cleaning — high-demand industries

IndustryApplicationRecommended grade
Automotive parts Deburring, descaling and weld slag removal for die-castings, forgings and stampings; pre-painting treatment G30 / G45
Steel structures, bridges, construction steel Weld seam grinding, rust removal, pre- and post-welding cleaning G30 / G45 plus high-carbon steel grit G25 / G40 in a 7:3 ratio
Shipbuilding and offshore engineering Rust removal on large steel plates, old paint stripping, cleaning of welded sections G30 / G45 plus high-carbon steel grit G40

Grinding steel shot for cleaning — moderate-demand industries

IndustryApplicationRecommended grade
Steel mills and heat treatment plants Scale removal after rolling or heat treatment G30 / G45 — any replacement must be justified with per-ton consumption data
Hardware and machining Deburring castings and forgings, mould cleaning, component pre-treatment G45 / G60 — specification requirements are diverse
Home appliances and white goods Pre-treatment of metal casings and brackets G60 — focus on controlling dust and colour variation
Maintenance and remanufacturing Refurbishing used parts, rust removal during equipment repair G30 / G45 — available in small-package supply

Grinding steel shot for peening — shot peening

Core function: impact-induced residual compressive stress improves fatigue life.

ApplicationRecommended grade
Spring manufacturing Automotive suspension springs, heavy-duty industrial springs → G70 / 82B
Rail transit wheels and high-speed train wheels Surface strengthening and crack propagation prevention → G70 / 9mm
Critical connection components for construction and engineering machinery Fatigue and brittleness resistance for gears, heavy-duty rods and bolts → G82B / 9mm

Choosing between G70 and G82B — they are not interchangeable

  • G70 (70# material, 50-55 HRC) sits in the cleaning band. It is a medium-duty option, appropriate where the peening requirement is genuine but light.
  • G82B (H grade, 55-60 HRC) is the material we lead with for heavy-duty peening, and the correct choice for safety-critical components such as suspension springs and train wheels.
  • Grinding steel shot is specified by material first, then roundness. Roundness grades G1-G3 are a suffix on the material designation, not a substitute for it.

Why so many different metal abrasives? Start from total cost

Total surface treatment cost is not the abrasive price. It is:

Total surface treatment cost = abrasive procurement cost + equipment wear cost + energy cost + labour and downtime cost + rework and scrap cost + environmental treatment cost

Abrasive procurement accounts for only 35-40% of the total. That means low-priced abrasives drive up the remaining 60-65% of costs — through faster media breakdown, higher energy draw, more dust, more downtime and more rework.

This is why the correct comparison between two grades is never price per kilogram. It is cost per ton of workpiece.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers drawn from the technical content above.

Which grinding steel shot grade should I use for general cleaning?
G30 or G45. They are made from 30# and 45# steel wire respectively, and cover the majority of cleaning work: deburring, descaling, weld slag removal and pre-painting treatment. G60 is used where a finer finish or tighter dust control is needed, such as home appliance casings.
What is the difference between the cleaning grades G30, G45, G60 and G70?
They are different base-material grades rather than a single hardness scale: G30 and G45 come from 30# and 45# steel wire and are the general purpose cleaning grades, G60 is used for finer cleaning where dust and surface appearance matter, and G70 (from 70# wire, 50-55 HRC) sits at the boundary between cleaning and peening.
Which grade should I use for shot peening?
For heavy-duty peening the preferred material is G82B (55-60 HRC). Automotive suspension springs, industrial heavy-duty springs, rail transit and high-speed train wheels, and fatigue-critical gears, heavy rods and bolts are all specified in G82B, with 9mm being the representative particle size for wheels and large components.
Is G70 suitable for peening?
G70 is a 50-55 HRC grade that sits in the cleaning band. It can be offered as a medium-duty solution where the peening requirement is genuine but light. For heavy-duty peening and any safety-critical component such as suspension springs or train wheels, G82B at 55-60 HRC is the correct choice.
Why does abrasive choice affect total cost so much?
Because abrasive procurement is only 35-40% of the total surface treatment cost. The remainder - equipment wear, energy, labour and downtime, rework and scrap, and environmental treatment - is driven mostly by how the media performs. Low-priced media push up that remaining 60-65%.
How do I replace an existing media grade without losing throughput?
Do not switch on price alone. Run the trial against per-ton consumption data from your own line: consumption in kg per ton of workpiece is the only figure that compares two media honestly, because it folds in breakage rate, dust load and rework.

Grinding steel shot grades follow the material-first convention: G30/G45/G60 for cleaning, G70 at 50-55 HRC for the cleaning-to-peening boundary, G82B at 55-60 HRC for heavy-duty peening. Steel shot and grit grades elsewhere on this site follow the GP 42-52 HRC / GL 52-61 HRC / GH above 61 HRC letter-grade scale.
