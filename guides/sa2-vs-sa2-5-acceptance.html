Sa 2 vs Sa 2½: on-site acceptance standards for rust removal

The two grades look similar until the day of acceptance. This is what the standard actually says, the visual test that settles a dispute on site, the three misconceptions that cost money, and where each grade is permitted.

By Hannibal Zhang, FENGERDA Updated 19 Oct 2026 Source: detecting...

Two notations, one grade. ISO 8501-1 writes Sa 2 and Sa 2½. Chinese project documents commonly write them as Sa2.0 and Sa2.5. They are the same two grades, and both spellings are accepted on this site so that a specification written either way can be matched to the right requirement.

Why so many projects end up in dispute

In steel structure anti-corrosion engineering, the rust removal grade directly determines the service life of the coating. Disputes are frequent: budget pressure pushes toward Sa2.0 to cut cost, while supervisors and owners insist on Sa2.5. Too many projects then suffer short-term coating blistering and peeling because the grade definition was left ambiguous — and the rework costs far more than the original saving on rust removal.

Many on-site managers cannot reliably distinguish the two grades and rely on rough visual judgement, which is where acceptance disputes come from.

Three prerequisites explain most of the confusion:

  1. Sa grades are assessed against the original rust grade of the steel (A, B, C, D). Even at the same removal grade, different substrates will not look identical.
  2. Rust removal grades define cleanliness only, not surface roughness. Many on-site projects confuse the two.
  3. Low-priced projects often involve opportunistic practice. The surface may look close to Sa2.5 while actually only reaching Sa2.0. Nothing appears wrong immediately, but rust penetration shows up after one to two years of coating service.

I. Core differences in the standard definitions

GradeStandard definition
Sa2.0
(Sa 2)
Thorough blasting		 The steel surface should be free of visible grease, dirt, loosely adhered scale, rust and coating residue. Tightly adhered scale and rust spots are permissible; these residues cannot be removed with a simple scraper.
Sa2.5
(Sa 2½)
Very thorough blasting		 The steel surface should be free of visible grease, dirt, scale, rust and old coatings. Only minor, dotted or streaky stains are permissible; large areas of firmly adhered material are not allowed.

II. Key points for on-site visual identification

GradeAppearance characteristic
Sa2.0 The overall substrate is exposed, but localised patches of dark rust and oxide scale spots remain. The substrate exhibits uneven light and dark distribution, with large areas of grey and dark brown patches.
Sa2.5 The substrate shows a uniform metallic luster; colour spots are limited to scattered dots and fine streaks, without continuous residue. This is the fastest and most practical criterion for on-site acceptance.
The single most useful inspection rule: look for continuous dark rust patches. Continuous residue means the surface has not reached Sa2.5, regardless of how bright the surrounding area looks.

III. Selecting the anti-corrosion system without mismatch

GradeWhere it appliesTypical system
Sa2.0 Indoor dry environments, temporary steel structures, low anti-corrosion requirements, short-term protection systems with a design service life of 3-5 years. Not suitable for humid, coastal or chemically corrosive environments. Thin-coat primer
Sa2.5 Wind turbine towers, ships, storage tanks, municipal steel structures, pipelines and outdoor heavy-duty anti-corrosion projects. The common standard for most formal heavy-duty projects. Thick-film primers, zinc-rich primers, epoxy heavy-duty anti-corrosion systems

IV. Common acceptance misconceptions

Misconception 1: a metallic sheen indicates Sa2.5

A sheen does not guarantee compliance. Continuous residual rust spots indicate Sa2.0, not Sa2.5.

Misconception 2: sanding Sa2.0 and applying a reinforcing primer can replace Sa2.5

Zinc-rich coatings are extremely sensitive to substrate cleanliness. A stable rust layer remaining in Sa2.0 can easily cause interface failure of the coating.

Misconception 3: increasing the blasting pressure will upgrade Sa2.0 to Sa2.5

Insufficient pressure, unsuitable abrasive particle size and simply extending the blasting time will not remove firmly adhered rust.

Key takeaways

  • Sa2.5 is the mainstream rust removal grade for heavy-duty anti-corrosion projects — the preferred grade for heavily corrosive outdoor, storage tank, wind power and steel structure work.
  • Sa2.0 permits continuous, firmly adhered rust residue and suits only dry, low-corrosion indoor environments. It cannot arbitrarily replace Sa2.5.
  • Rust removal grade is a cleanliness standard. Surface roughness must be checked separately during acceptance. The two cannot substitute for each other.
  • Bidding and construction technical plans must state the rust removal grade in writing to avoid disputes at later acceptance.

For the argument on the next step up the ladder — whether Sa 2½ is itself over-specified — see Sa 2½ vs. Sa 3: when does "cleaner" stop paying off?

Practical on-site suggestions

  1. Before project commencement, create and retain standard Sa2.0 and Sa2.5 sample plates as a unified acceptance reference for all parties.
  2. The blasting team should carry out sample acceptance on arrival, to confirm the abrasive type, blasting distance and pressure parameters.
  3. During inspections, supervisors should focus on whether continuous dark rust patches are present on the steel surface. Continuous residue means Sa2.5 has not been met.
  4. For projects with a design life greater than 5 years, or in humid, salt spray or chemical media environments, the technical specification strictly prohibits the use of Sa2.0.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers drawn from the technical content above.

What is the difference between Sa2.0 and Sa2.5 sandblasting?
Sa2.0 (written Sa 2 in ISO 8501-1) is thorough blasting: the surface must be free of visible grease, dirt, loosely adhered scale, rust and coating residue, but tightly adhered scale and rust spots are permissible because they cannot be removed with a simple scraper. Sa2.5 (ISO 8501-1 writes Sa 2 1/2) is very thorough blasting: the surface must be free of all visible grease, dirt, scale, rust and old coatings, with only minor dotted or streaky stains permissible and no large areas of firmly adhered material.
How do I judge Sa2.0 versus Sa2.5 visually on site?
Sa 2 shows the substrate exposed but with localised patches of dark rust and oxide scale remaining, uneven light and dark distribution, large areas of grey and dark brown patches. Sa 2 1/2 shows a uniform metallic luster with colour spots limited to scattered dots and fine streaks and no continuous residue. This is the fastest and most practical acceptance criterion.
Does a metallic sheen mean the surface meets Sa2.5?
No. A sheen does not guarantee compliance. Continuous residual rust spots indicate Sa 2, not Sa 2 1/2. Inspect for continuous dark rust patches: if they are present, the surface has not reached Sa 2 1/2.
Can I sand Sa2.0 and apply a reinforcing primer instead of blasting to Sa2.5?
No. Zinc-rich coatings are extremely sensitive to substrate cleanliness. A stable rust layer remaining in Sa 2 can easily cause interface failure of the coating, which is exactly the kind of early blistering and peeling that rework then costs more than the original savings.
Will increasing blasting pressure upgrade Sa2.0 to Sa2.5?
No. Insufficient pressure, unsuitable abrasive particle size and simply extending blasting time will not remove firmly adhered rust. The limit is in the adhesion of the residue and the abrasive selected, not in pressure alone.
When is Sa2.0 acceptable, and when is it prohibited?
Sa 2 suits indoor dry environments, temporary steel structures, low anti-corrosion requirements and short-term protection systems with a design life of 3-5 years. Sa 2 1/2 suits wind turbine towers, ships, storage tanks, municipal steel structures, pipelines and outdoor heavy-duty projects, paired with thick-film primers, zinc-rich primers and epoxy heavy-duty systems. For design life over 5 years, or humid, salt spray or chemical environments, technical specifications strictly prohibit Sa 2.

Grades are as defined in ISO 8501-1, written Sa 2 and Sa 2½; the common project spellings Sa2.0 and Sa2.5 refer to the same two grades. Visual acceptance remains a matter of trained judgement — where the contract requires it, specify a reference sample plate and a formal comparison method.
