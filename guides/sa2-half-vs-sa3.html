Sa 2½ vs. Sa 3: when does "cleaner" stop paying off?

Specifying a higher cleanliness grade than your coating requires is one of the most common and most expensive mistakes in surface preparation.

By Hannibal Zhang, FENGERDA Updated 19 Oct 2026 Source: detecting...

ISO 8501-1 defines four blast-cleaning grades. Specifying a higher grade than your coating requires is one of the most common — and most expensive — mistakes in surface preparation.

When each grade is right

  • Sa 1 (light): Maintenance blasting, removing loose paint and rust before recoating. Rarely specified for new work.
  • Sa 2 (thorough): General maintenance, non-critical structural steel, shop primer application.
  • Sa 2½ (very thorough): The industry default for most coating systems — epoxy primers, zinc-rich primers, polyurethane topcoats. 90% of new structural steel spec is Sa 2½.
  • Sa 3 (visually clean steel): Required only for thermal spraying (metallizing), high-performance tank linings, and some nuclear/aerospace applications. Demands clean, dry abrasive and a controlled environment.

Why over-specifying burns budget

  • Media consumption doubles going from Sa 2½ to Sa 3 — you blast the same surface twice to remove the last "shadows."
  • Roughness climbs — Sa 3 on a profile-sensitive coating (e.g. thin-film epoxy) can push Rz above the coating's tolerance, causing pinholes.
  • Reblast risk — if the surface flash-rusts before coating (especially outdoors), you reblast to Sa 3 again, doubling cost.

The decision rule

Always spec the lowest grade your coating supplier will accept. If the data sheet says "Sa 2½, Rz 50-80 μm," do not upgrade to Sa 3 "to be safe" — it adds 40-60% to your blasting cost with zero coating benefit. Reserve Sa 3 for thermal spray and critical tank linings where the spec demands it.

Bottom line: Check the coating data sheet first, spec the grade it requires, and confirm with your abrasive supplier that your media + equipment can hit that grade + profile consistently.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers drawn from the technical content above.

What is the difference between Sa 2, Sa 2 1/2 and Sa 3?
ISO 8501-1 defines four blast-cleaning grades. Sa 1 is light blasting for maintenance, Sa 2 is thorough blasting for general maintenance and shop primer, Sa 2 1/2 is very thorough blasting and the industry default for most coating systems, and Sa 3 is visually clean steel required for thermal spraying and high-performance tank linings.
Which grade do most coating systems require?
Sa 2 1/2 is the industry default for epoxy primers, zinc-rich primers and polyurethane topcoats - about 90% of new structural steel specifications are Sa 2 1/2.
When is Sa 3 actually required?
Sa 3 is required for thermal spraying (metallizing), high-performance tank linings and some nuclear or aerospace applications, and it demands clean, dry abrasive and a controlled environment.
What does over-specifying from Sa 2 1/2 to Sa 3 cost?
Media consumption roughly doubles, because you blast the same surface twice to remove the last shadows, and it adds about 40-60% to blasting cost with no coating benefit. Roughness can also climb above a thin-film coating's Rz tolerance and cause pinholes.
What is the correct decision rule for specifying a grade?
Always specify the lowest grade your coating supplier will accept. If the data sheet says Sa 2 1/2, Rz 50-80 um, do not upgrade to Sa 3 to be safe. Reserve Sa 3 for thermal spray and critical tank linings where the spec demands it.
What should you confirm before ordering media for a given grade?
Confirm with your abrasive supplier that your media and equipment can hit the specified grade and roughness profile consistently, and check the coating data sheet first.
