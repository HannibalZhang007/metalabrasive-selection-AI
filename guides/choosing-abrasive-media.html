How to choose the right abrasive media for your application

Six questions, answered in order: base material, surface treatment, target roughness, equipment, hardness grade and annual throughput.

By Hannibal Zhang, FENGERDA Updated 19 Oct 2026 Source: detecting...

Choosing the right abrasive media comes down to six questions, answered in order:

1. What is your base material?

  • Ferrous (carbon steel, cast iron) — the widest media family is available: steel shot (A/B), steel grit (C/D), grinding shot (F/G/H/N) and recycled cut wire (E).
  • Stainless steel — you must use stainless shot (J/K/L/M) or stainless cut wire (O/P) to avoid carbon contamination that causes rust spots.
  • Non-ferrous (aluminum, copper, zinc) — copper cut wire (I) or stainless media; never carbon steel media (causes iron smut and galvanic corrosion).

2. What surface treatment do you need?

  • Cleaning / rust removal — spherical media (steel shot A, or stainless shot J for stainless bases).
  • Descaling / roughening — angular media (steel grit C, or bearing grit D for longer life).
  • Shot peening — spherical media only, to induce residual compressive stress; steel shot A for general parts, grinding shot G82B (H-grade) for heavy-duty peening.
  • Precision cleaning / polishing — fine spherical media, low power.

3. Target roughness Rz?

Rz determines particle size. For steel shot: S170 (<50 μm), S230 (50-80), S280 (50-100), S330/S390 (80-120+). For steel grit: G80 → G16 as roughness increases. Stainless and cut wire use diameter φ (0.4 mm → 1.4 mm).

4. Equipment type?

Wheel blasting (抛丸机) handles both shot and grit. Pressure blasting (喷砂机) with shot peening must use grit only — never shot or grinding shot (they jam and break the nozzle).

5. Hardness grade?

For steel shot/grit: GP (42-52 HRC, general), GL (52-61 HRC, shot peening), GH (>61 HRC, special high-wear). Stainless, copper, cut wire and grinding shot do not use this scale.

6. Annual throughput?

Multiply throughput (tons) × industry consumption rate (kg/ton) ÷ 1000 × recovery factor. With a recovery system the factor is 1; without it, 1.6 (you consume 60% more). ≥300 t/yr qualifies for on-site visit and custom blending.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers drawn from the technical content above.

What is the right abrasive media for carbon steel and cast iron?
Carbon steel and cast iron are ferrous, so the widest media family is available: steel shot (A/B), steel grit (C/D), grinding shot (F/G/H/N) and recycled cut wire (E).
Which media must be used on stainless steel, and why?
Stainless steel requires stainless shot (J/K/L/M) or stainless cut wire (O/P). Carbon contamination from carbon steel media causes rust spots on stainless parts.
Can steel shot be used in a pressure blasting machine?
No. Pressure blasting with shot peening must use angular grit only. Spherical shot and grinding steel shot can jam and break the nozzle in a pressure blasting system.
How do I convert target roughness Rz into particle size?
Rz determines particle size. For steel shot: S170 (below 50 um), S230 (50-80), S280 (50-100), S330/S390 (80-120 and above). For steel grit: G80 down to G16 as roughness increases. Stainless and cut wire are specified by diameter, from 0.4 mm to 1.4 mm.
How do GP, GL and GH hardness grades differ?
For steel shot and grit, GP is 42-52 HRC for general-purpose blasting, GL is 52-61 HRC for shot peening, and GH is above 61 HRC for special high-wear applications. Stainless, copper, cut wire and grinding shot do not use this scale.
How is annual abrasive consumption calculated?
Multiply throughput in tons by the industry consumption rate in kg per ton, divide by 1000, then apply a recovery factor: 1 with a media recovery system, or 1.6 without one because you consume 60% more. Annual usage of 300 tons or more qualifies for an on-site visit and custom blending.
