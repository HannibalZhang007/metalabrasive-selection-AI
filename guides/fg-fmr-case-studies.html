What FG and FMR are, and what they delivered

FG is a high-silicon, high-manganese steel shot and FMR is a chromium-molybdenum steel shot. Together they are Fengerda's high-end series, engineered to deliver imported-grade performance at a China-source total-cost-of-ownership advantage.

Results across the three case studies below

  • Abrasive consumption down 20-30%
  • Blasting / descaling speed up 20-25%
  • Dust emission down about 35%
  • Energy and wheel load down about 10-12%
  • Second-pass re-blast largely eliminated on qualified stock

Case 1 - US international manufacturer (founded 1991)

Sector: industrial component manufacturing, descaling and surface preparation.

Challenge: persistently high abrasive consumption and inconsistent cleaning results across shifts. Quality drift forced slow-downs and rework.

Approach: on-site diagnosis plus system match

A Fengerda application engineer ran an NSRP-style line audit covering blasting-wheel current, abrasive mix ratio, metering valve, air pressure, separator efficiency and dust-collector differential. The finding was that consumption was driven more by system mismatch than by the abrasive itself. The abrasive programme was re-designed around high-quality steel shot, and wheel current and separator settings were then co-optimised with the customer's team.

Results, verified over 12 months

  • Abrasive consumption down 25%+
  • Direct purchasing cost saved over USD 150,000 in the first year
  • Sa 2½ first-pass yield improved — fewer re-blasts, less rework
  • Dust emission down about 35% (low-fines formulation)
  • Wheel-motor load and energy down, tracked via on-load current mapping
  • TCO reduction in line with the 25%+ total-cost benchmark seen across premium-abrasive programmes

Why this matters to buyers: the win came from abrasive plus parameter co-optimisation, not a price cut — the same "stop buying by the kilogram, buy by cost per tonne of workpiece" logic global mills now expect.

Case 2 - one of Asia's largest shipbuilders

Sector: hull block and panel shot-blasting prior to coating.

Challenge: high total abrasive consumption and variable blasting efficiency on the panel line threatened coating adhesion and throughput.

Approach

The shot-to-grit mix ratio was rebalanced and Fengerda's newly patented FG / FMR product was deployed on the blasting line, stabilised through AI Smart Gradation™ so the blend stays inside a tight specification shift to shift.

Results

  • Total abrasive consumption down about 30%
  • Blasting efficiency up 20% — more panels per shift
  • Sa 2½ first-pass yield up, so fewer re-blasts before painting
  • Dust down about 35%, improving the working environment and cutting hazardous-waste handling
  • Coverage consistency improved, giving an anchored profile for coating adhesion

Why this matters to buyers: this mirrors the global shift to shot-grit hybrid blends that release line capacity — the same mechanism behind documented 50%+ cycle-time and 100%+ capacity gains in European shot-grit trials — delivered with a China-source cost structure.

Case 3 - integrated steel mill plate and section descaling line

Sector: continuous shot-blasting of plates and sections for descaling before coating or galvanising.

Customer demands, in the industry's own words: raise surface cleanliness, lower roughness deviation, cut abrasive consumption, protect equipment, reduce dust.

Approach: side-by-side pilot

On a descaling line running standard alloy shot, a side-by-side pilot was run in two steps:

  1. Switched to FG / FMR shot (55-62 HRC, 40% better hardness stability) plus companion steel grit (Mohs 9.0+, +25% cleaning speed) in an optimised ratio.
  2. Audited wheel current, separator and metering valve — the same levers that cut energy about 12% and consumption about 17% in documented European trials.

Representative results

Pilot basis: one line, one-shift baseline against the trial.

  • Abrasive consumption down 20% to 30%
  • Descaling and cleaning speed up 20% to 25%
  • Roughness deviation reduced, giving a more uniform anchor profile and better coating or galvanising adhesion
  • Dust emission down 35% (lower fines), with hazardous waste reduced
  • On-load wheel current and energy down about 10-12%, containing equipment wear
  • Secondary re-blast largely eliminated on qualified stock

The TCO model behind all three cases

Unit cost per square metre of treated area = (abrasive consumption rate × unit price + disposal) + labour + energy and equipment — all divided by treated area.

When a higher-grade abrasive simultaneously lowers A (consumption), Cd (disposal), D (labour and rework) and E (energy and wear), the unit-price premium is absorbed in the total cost. That is the "quality abrasive pays for itself" effect.
